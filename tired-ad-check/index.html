The Tired-Ad Check

Free to forward, print or post.
No sign-up.

Cost per purchase creeping up every week? Before you make new ads, find out which ads are actually worn out, which are losing for another reason, and what the replacements have to beat. About 15 minutes in Ads Manager.

1Build a “Fatigue check” column preset once, 5 min · Ads tab → Columns → Customize

  • Delivery shows Meta’s own “Creative limited / Creative fatigue” labels, where available1
  • Amount spent: fatigue on an ad with 2% of spend doesn’t matter; on one with 40%, it’s the whole problem
  • Frequency (impressions ÷ reach), read per ad, not per campaign: averages hide one overexposed ad4
  • CPM separates “this ad is tiring” from “the auction got expensive”5
  • Link CTR and CPC, the earliest sign people are scrolling past an ad they’ve seen6
  • Purchases, cost per purchase, and purchase conversion rate (purchases ÷ link clicks)

2Compare the right weeks 5 min

  • Last 7 days, with Compare to previous 7 days on. Fatigue shows up as change, not one bad number.
  • Sort by amount spent. Work only on the top 5–10 ads.
  • For each one, note its numbers from its first 14 days live. That’s its own baseline.
  • Breakdown → By time → Week. Frequency rising while CTR falls is the classic fatigue shape6.

Your bar. For each ad you marked “fatigue”, write down its last-7-day cost per purchase. A new ad beats it when it brings purchases at a lower cost than that. Why it’s worth the 15 minutes: in Meta’s own analysis, by four repeat exposures the likelihood of a conversion was about 45% lower, in their model3.
